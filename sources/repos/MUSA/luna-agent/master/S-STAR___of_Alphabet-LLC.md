## 6. Three depths of sameness — P=NP, P==NP, P===NP

His paper's title is three questions deep. Defer to computation, not abstract math:
each depth asks what can be *built*, and at what cost the answer can be *checked*.

- **`=` — assignment.** The assignable layer. To ask whether something is assignable
  is to ask whether a binding can be constructed: does there exist a construction
  that puts this value in this place? Assignable, not equitable — it is about the
  *act* of binding, not fairness of outcome. Cost: the cost of the construction.
- **`==` — equivalence.** The construct of equal types, equal measure, equal value.
  Two things are `==` when they agree under a stated measure — same type, same
  reading, same worth *by that measure*. It is a relation, and relations need their
  measure declared. Cost: the cost of the measure.
- **`===` — identity.** The third three: indistinguishable under *every* operation
  of the substrate. Not equal by measure — the same under all probes. Cost:
  unbounded in general, which is exactly why it is the deepest question.

So: **P=NP** asks whether feasibility is *assignable* to problems;
**P==NP** asks whether feasibility is *equivalent* across models of computation;
**P===NP** asks whether the two classes are *identical* in the substrate — and the
three questions have three different costs of answering. That is the computational
problem, and it is why the title has three depths.
