## I. What we are naming

### Historical systems, not invented history

1. **Arabic abjad**: writing system with contextual joining behavior.
2. **Rasm (الرسم)**: the underlying consonantal written shape/skeleton.
3. **Iʿjām (الإعجام)**: letter-distinguishing consonant dots.
4. **Naqṭ al-iʿrāb / ḍabṭ / tashkīl**: vocalization and related performance/grammatical marks, represented differently across historical manuscripts.
5. **Ihmāl (الإهمال)**: lack of consonant-distinguishing marks in appropriate orthographic conventions.
6. **Our new computational designation:** **Rasm-State Alphabet (RSA)**, a *versioned, contextual quotient alphabet*, not an assertion that the script was universally or permanently undotted.

Manuscript evidence establishes that red dots often recorded vowel information in early Qurʾān copies; different uses of color and consonant marks developed over time. Not every early manuscript is wholly unpointed. No credible historical evidence found assigns the development of Arabic pointing to alchemical depictions of valence electrons. Do not encode that as fact. Source: Nick Posegay, *Journal of Near Eastern Studies* (2021), https://doi.org/10.1086/712876; see also museum examples https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/449214 and https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_2001-0605-0-1.

### Do not confuse particles

* `phoneme`: contrastive speech-language sound category.
* `phone`: realized sound; `phonetic-feature vector`: organized attributes of articulation, manner and other features.
* `phonon`: quasiparticle of vibrational excitation in condensed-matter physics; it is **not** the name for an Arabic letter's phonetic component.
* `photon`: quantum of electromagnetic field.
* `Q-String`: our *classical typed relational record* connecting graphs/strings with provenance, unless an actual Hilbert-space physics model is supplied separately.

The technical name for the proposed string representation is **Articulatory Feature String (AFS)** or **Ṣifah-Vector String (SVS)**; 'phonon string' could be a house alias only with an explicit disclaimer.

## II. First formalism: contextual fibers of the rasm

Let `A` be the base 28-letter alphabet **of the current Timeless model**, `P={initial,medial,final,isolated}`, and `S_p` the chosen skeleton glyph inventory at position `p`. The existing code registers a total map:

`r_p: A -> S_p`.

Its **fiber** at skeleton glyph `s` is `r_p^{-1}(s) = {a in A: r_p(a)=s}`. Defining the relation `a~_p b` iff `r_p(a)=r_p(b)` makes the contextual alphabet a *quotient set* `A/~_p`.

This is the answer to the question 'What is a single undotted letter whose daughters are individually dotted letters?' **A contextual graphemic equivalence class / rasm allograph fiber**. Unlike a new 15-letter natural-language alphabet, this is a mathematical alphabet generated by an explicitly chosen historical/typographic orthography model.

The repository's MEDIAL table groups 28 letters into 15 classes. Notable classes:

* `ٮ -> {ب,ت,ث,ن,ي}` in medial and initial positions (five alternatives).
* `ٮ -> {ب,ت,ث}` in final and isolated positions (three alternatives); final `ن` and `ي` have distinct models `ں`, `ى`.
* `ح -> {ج,ح,خ}`; `ٯ -> {ف,ق}` in this engine's model.
* Singletons `ا,ك,ل,م,ه,و` **under this specific simplified inventory**; presentation-form era, manuscript style and calligraphic variants require independent scrutiny.

**Warning:** Word shape is not a simple sequence of context-free letter replacements. The joining behavior of neighboring letters determines shape, and fonts can render contextual glyphs differently. Historically authored manuscript styles may require per-era projections, and a normalization schema must not silently substitute Unicode `ى` (alif maqsūra) for a generic dotless-yāʾ identity without disambiguation.

For a sequence with contextual positions `p_i` and skeleton glyphs `s_i`, let

`Ω(s)=Π_i r_{p_i}^{-1}(s_i)`.

Then `D(s)=|Ω(s)|` counts *combinatorially admissible consonant fillings*, not actual dictionary words or historical readings. In the code snapshot, `كتب` projects to `كٮٮ` with slot sizes `(1,5,3)`: `D=15`. The Hartley cardinality measure is `H0=log2(15)=3.9068905956 bits`. That is not empirical Shannon entropy unless a uniform distribution over these 15 fillings is justified. More broadly, real word probability depends on morphology, corpus, dialect, epoch and context.

**Formal reconstruction law:** projection is many-to-one; inverse is `r^{-1}(s)` as a **set-valued preimage**. A unique restoration requires external recorded dots, dictionary and context evidence, or another witness. A hash is not inherently reversible.

## III. Constraint algebra: from dots to meaningful words

The skeleton's Cartesian expansion must pass evidence-bearing constraints:

`C0(s)=Ω(s)` (orthographic fillings)

`C1(s)=C0(s) ∩ Lexicon(epoch,dialect,register)`

`C2(s)=C1(s) ∩ Morphology(pattern,features)`

`C3(s)=C2(s) ∩ Syntax(context)`

`C4(s)=C3(s) ∩ Semantic/DiscourseEvidence(context)`.

**Never** infer that `|C4|=0` means the manuscript is corrupt: it might mean the lookup dictionary is incomplete, the dialect unsupported, a proper noun occurs, or the filters themselves conflict. A zero state is **inconsistency relative to the declared model**. A true manuscript damage diagnosis requires further evidence. An unresolved candidate set of size >1 is not proven to be *intended ambiguity*; intent requires evidence, which may remain inaccessible.

The old `spec/05-superposition.md` says partial bindings form a lattice. There is a subtle correction: if conflicting bindings at the same slot are both singleton constraints, their intersection is empty. To obtain a proper lattice, include **bottom = ∅** (contradictory binding) and permit arbitrary subsets of the positional domains, or model the partially specified objects as a join-semilattice when contradictions are not represented. Use constraint sets ordered by inclusion; meet is intersection; join is union. This is mathematically closed.

QIA's 'superposition' denotes an **explicit set or support of classical candidate states**, with optional evidence-weighted probability distribution. It does not entail coherent quantum superposition, amplitudes, interference or wavefunction collapse.

## IV. Second formalism: phonological/articulatory feature channels

The Arabic Timeless Layer 17 source directly establishes **five general articulation zones and a particular 17-position school of classification**:

* الجوف — cavity: 1 traditional category for madd long vowels.
* الحلق — throat: 3.
* اللسان — tongue: 10.
* الشفتان — lips: 2, namely `ف` lower lip against upper incisors, and `ب م و` bilabial articulation in their relevant configurations.
* الخيشوم — nasal passage: 1 for ghunnah, not an independent consonantal letter.

The counted 17 are tied to Ibn al-Jazarī's well-known account; historically other scholars count 14 or 16, so **classify by scholarly system** rather than promoting 17 as a scientific invariant of human anatomy. `ء` must have its own phonemic identity even though the current 28-letter written model's `ARTICULATION` map does not separately carry it. `و` and `ي` vary by whether one is modeling a consonant, glide or vowel.

Classical Arabic ṣifāt (letter properties) are best encoded as features; do not conflate them with modern IPA voice-onset or laryngeal phonetics without empirical mapping. A useful per-phoneme typed object is:

`PhonemeFeature = (phoneme_id, inventory_version, articulatory_system, zone, exact_point, manner, nasal, shiddah, jahr, hams, rikhawa, tawassut, isti'la, istifal, itbaq, infitah, idhlaq, ismat, safir, qalqala, ...)`.

Opposite-class categories are **partition claims** that must be verified per tradition, not binary assumptions about every articulatory feature. Some attributes are tri-valued, e.g. shiddah / tawassut (bayniyyah) / rikhawah; qalqala is not a mere Boolean function of two other attributes.

Let classical `Shiddah={ء,أ (one phoneme),ج,د,ق,ط,ب,ك,ت}` and `Jahr` be the complement of the ten traditionally hams-class letters in the extended consonant inventory. Their intersection, counting hamza once, is:

`Jahr ∩ Shiddah = {ء, ب, ج, د, ق, ط}`.

The accepted classical **qalqala class is `{ق,ط,ب,ج,د}`**, five letters. Thus

`Qalqala = (Jahr ∩ Shiddah) \ {ء}`

is a useful *observed set identity under this taxonomy*, **not** an etymological proof that qalqala is caused solely by those two feature labels. Hamza's exclusion is a necessary separate rule. Also distinguish possessing the categorical qalqala attribute from its audible realization in the appropriate vowel/sukūn contexts. Sources: https://shamela.ws/book/38104/74, https://www.islamweb.net/ar/fatwa/120034, https://www.alukah.net/sharia/0/89723.

### Source-to-feature graph

Connect a letter to its exact point, then to zone, while independently linking each letter to contrasting/classical ṣifāt features. This creates multiple admissible paths:

`letter -> {specific point, zone, sifa vector, phoneme, written variants, lexical roots}`.

A feature distance `d_F(a,b)` is only defined once the feature coding/weights are chosen and validated. Equal zone does not imply equal point, and equal point does not imply similar meanings. Candidate spelling transformations are *hypotheses to look up*, never etymological/semantic discoveries from articulation alone.

## V. The requested `كتب` / `كتم` demonstration

Correct the exact source surface first:

* `كَتَبَ` (*kataba*) = he wrote; root `ك ت ب`; undiacritized `كتب`.
* `كَتَمَ` (*katama*) = he concealed, kept hidden; root `ك ت م`; undiacritized `كتم`.
* `كَتَبَة`/`كَتَبَةٌ` and related plural spellings are not the same lexical item as `كَتَبَ` merely because a final `ة` was added; `كَتَبَة` is a plural noun for scribes in relevant vocalizations/lexica.

The transformation `كتب -> كتم` is **NOT an iʿjām-only change**. The third glyph `ب` and `م` do not belong to the same final rasm fiber; `كٮٮ ≠ كٮم`. It **IS an articulation-point-preserving consonant substitution** in the cited Timeless 17-point classical model: both `ب` and `م` map to point 15 (`الشفتان`). There are non-trivial ṣifāt differences: `ب` is a stop/shadīd, `م` is nasal and not a shadīd consonant in the same sense; `ب` is listed among qalqala, `م` is not. In IPA-inspired terms, they differ in manner and nasal airflow; those modern measurements require a separately declared inventory.

Thus the Q-string is:

`QString(source='كتب', target='كتم', relation='same_articulatory_point_substitution', chart='Arabic Timeless 17 point system', evidence='root forms are attested separately', claims={not_rasm_preserving:true, shared_exact_point:true, semantic_relationship:unproved})`.

**Do not infer** that concealment is a semantic consequence of replacing `ب` with `م`. That hypothesis requires a controlled study against negative pairs and independent dictionaries, and the alternative may simply be unrelated root semantics. Another caution: `كبت` is a real Arabic root/word meaning suppression/repression; one earlier Timeless specification treats it as if lexical rejection were immediate, so corpus-backed checking is required.

## VI. A WAZNOMIA construction beyond one-way morphology

Muse's attached WAZNOMIA header offers two tasks:

* `NAME_DETERMINE`: name -> candidate segmented root/operator -> candidate historical/function semantics;
* `FUNCTION_DETERMINE`: typed pure function -> candidate nomenclatures, constrained by language, formation patterns and evidence.

Preserve her central proposal **as a research hypothesis**, while replacing absolute universal claims with *set-valued attestable analysis*: many technical terms are loanwords, proper names, metaphorical shifts, calques, irregular formations and ambiguous roots. Root+pattern processing is often noninjective. Cross-language etymological agreement creates a testable correspondence; it does NOT by itself verify independent scientific identities.

A WAZNOMIA derivation object:

`Derivation = (surface, language, epoch, region, sense_id, root_candidates, pattern_candidates, affix_analysis, attested_translations, reconstructed_function_candidates, uncertainty, sources, counterexamples, operations, normalization_loss)`.

Comparative Latin, Greek, and Arabic etymology are distinct analyzed layers. Never derive scientific truth from semantic resemblance alone. Arabic Form VI تفاعل is a common relational/reciprocal formation, but that is a broad morphological tendency not a theorem that all reciprocal scientific concepts are Form VI, or that all Form VI words are mutual in every lexicalized usage.

## VII. The Q-String typed hypergraph

A mere weighted edge between two words is insufficient: this is a **multiway witnessed claim**:

`e=(participants:[node IDs], roles:[source,target,chart,source text], relationType, guard, opSignature, proofOrWitness, epistemic, context, version)`.

Define edges for:

`same_rasm_in_position`, `ijam_variant`, `same_articulation_zone`, `same_articulation_point`, `feature_contrast`, `morphological_derivation`, `syntagmatic_governance`, `lexical_sense`, `translation_attestation`, `etymological_origin`, `analogy_only`, `hypothesis`, `contradiction`.

**Address = `qstr:v1:SHA256(canonical serialized payload)`**, with side-car operation registry. Source-reference hash is distinct from asserted semantic truth. Never put executable instructions into a hash's mathematical preimage; effectful operations require authorization and a typed safe interpreter. Lookup expected O(1) for a good in-memory hash index is NOT O(1) for traversing or enumerating a graph; runtime, memory, corpus-index build costs must be measured.

## VIII. The crucial existing Timeless normalization risk

The original repository (`lib/engine/variants.ts`, `text.ts`) currently maps `أ إ آ ٱ` into `ا`, `ؤ` into `و`, `ئ` into `ي`, `ء` into `ا`, `ة` into `ه`, and `ى` into `ي`. The current engine is explicit that these are **projections** for studying 28 canonical source letters, but WAZNOMIA cannot assume that the originals remain uniquely reconstructible from that projection.

Proposed safe fix: *do not change the original source engine in this research phase*. Build a wrapper preserving:

```
RawSurface{utf8, unicode_nfc, codepoint_sequence, epoch, source_offset}
 -> OrthographicProjection{projection_id, version, loss_ledger}
 -> RasmState{glyphs, positional_slots, domain_products}
 -> PhonemicAnalysis{inventory, context, alternatives}
 -> MorphoLexicalAnalysis{root,pattern,lexeme,attestations}
 -> SemanticFunctionalAnalysis{evidence-graded function}
```

A historical manuscript's dots are *observed strokes or ink marks*; computational feature vectors are analyst-defined abstractions. Do not confuse the two.

## IX. Your proposed 48 or 40+ morphological states

There is no universally established one-size-fits-all number of Arabic 'weights' covering all roots, inflections, derivations, weak forms, dialects and nominal morphology. WAZNOMIA's 'forty and more' is a proposal for a particular operator inventory. Give every pattern an ID and explicit universe of discourse: `form_I..X`, further classical extensions, masdar templates, active/passive participles, diminutives, number/gender/person/aspect/mood/case/state features. Use source-backed labels rather than an asserted `48`.

CAMeL morphology already exposes precisely specified features including `root`, `pattern`, `lex`, `diac`, `asp`, `cas`, `gen`, `num`, `per`, `vox`, `mod`, and inflectional options: https://camel-tools.readthedocs.io/en/latest/reference/camel_morphology_features.html. This is a practical bridge from abstract WAZNOMIA operators to actual observable analyses.

Root-template features constrain candidate words; **letters' iʿjām dots are not mechanically changed by every ṣarf transformation**. Some derived patterns insert `ت`, `ا`, `م`, `س`, `ن`, alter vowels, assimilate radicals or trigger orthographic conventions. The effect on dot-state space must be obtained by *generating the result and re-projecting it*, not assigning an unsupported universal dot-operation to a semantic verb form.

## X. Exact cross-field mathematical operators

1. **Contextual quotient**: `A/~p` groups letters by common glyph under `r_p`.
2. **Fiber/preimage**: `r_p^-1(s)` yields all letter options for a glyph.
3. **Constraint lattice**: admit `∅` for contradictions; conjunction = intersection, disjunction = union (same typed slot domain).
4. **Hartley vs Shannon**: `H0=log2(D)` counts alternatives; `H(P)=-Σ p_i log2(p_i)` quantifies modeled uncertainty once the distribution is attested.
5. **Information gain**: `IG = H(C_before)-H(C_after)` if the same probability model is used and conditioning is valid.
6. **Commutator / operation order**: compare `F(G(x))` and `G(F(x))` only when both are well-typed; if incompatible, report **type mismatch**, not 'they do not commute'.
7. **Feature relations**: take intersections, set differences, and implication counterexamples with respect to a named inventory.
8. **Product machines**: combine finite-state morphological transducers with finite-state constraints, then independently verify lexical semantics.
9. **Formal concept lattice**: corpus incidence `(lexeme, attested property)` permits Galois closure; co-occurrence does not imply etymological causality.
10. **Tensor/factor graph**: a compact joint model may factor probabilities over orthography, morphology, syntax, region and epoch; benchmark exact, approximate and sparse traversal.
11. **Q-String provenance**: content address the witnessed relation; implement reversible serialization only if source payload is persisted.

## XI. Rigorous experimental protocol — before any open dataset mining

* Pre-register a task: orthographic candidate recall, contextual reading discrimination, morphology generation accuracy, phonetic-neighborhood lexical relation, or cross-language technical-name determination.
* Use separate **positive pairs** (known graphemic / morphological / etymological relationships) and **hard negatives** (same articulatory position yet different etymology; same rasm but not lexicalized; same numeric abjad weight but unrelated meaning; loanwords; words with multiple plausible roots).
* Split by root-family, document source and chronological period to prevent leakage across inflected surface forms.
* For hand-checks, build a tiny fixed lexicon; for scale, import vetted open data *only in the subsequent approved phase*.
* Compare exact rasm projection, dictionary-only lookup, feature-augmented inference, finite-state morphological analysis, and a no-feature control.
* Report precision/recall for candidate generation separately from precision@k for lexical ranking and exact match for morphological output.
* Report source evidence correctness, False Discovery Rate, ambiguity calibration, time/space cost and proof witness coverage.
* For historical assertions, an independently transcribed early manuscript with manuscript ID and era is the primary record; modern typed Arabic is not a substitute.

Potential resources: CAMeL Tools (`CAMeL-Lab/camel_tools`), CAMeL Morph (`CAMeL-Lab/camel_morph`), Quranic Arabic Corpus morphology with its own license, Wiktionary/Kaikki as noisy lexical *sources*, and CLTK for independently attested Greek/Latin linguistic analysis. Do not collect them silently as a prerequisite, and do not claim their existence establishes WAZNOMIA's universal theory.
