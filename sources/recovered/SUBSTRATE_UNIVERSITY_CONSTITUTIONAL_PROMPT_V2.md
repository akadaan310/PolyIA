# B. THE CINEMATIC CAMPUS — THE EXPERIENCE IS THE UNIVERSITY

## B1. Immediate first impression

On opening `/`, the visitor approaches an extraordinary university by an immersive animated camera sequence. This cannot be a hero stock image pasted behind a headline. Construct a navigable scene with original stylized-photoreal **Gothic-meets-futurist** architecture: layered black stone, monumental gates, long illuminated halls, deep courtyards, moving gold typographic inlays, mathematical reliefs, wet reflections, atmospheric depth, celestial objects, and visible doors representing the six divisions. Cinematic music/ambience is optional and requires a user gesture; muted mode must be excellent.

The introduction has no more than a few seconds of non-skippable delay: skip/enter controls, mouse/touch orbit and reduced-motion access appear immediately. A returning visitor may go directly to the location they last used. The visitor should be able to recognize a full university and interact with at least one door or constellation on the first viewport. A narrator/HM can welcome a newcomer using deterministic text and a small permitted glossary. Discovering how the campus works is part of the site.

## B2. A real spatial information architecture

The user traverses an original campus consisting at minimum of:

- **The Gate / Hall of First Principles** — mathematical threshold puzzles and world invitation.
- **The Atrium of Six Divisions** — six grand visually distinct doors, each opening a navigable realm with teaching objects, example exhibits, a subject glossary and a curriculum path.
- **The Library of Forms** — built shelves of publications and participant-created volumes; existing CGT book in a functioning reader; a number of demonstrator books with visible specimen status.
- **The Observatory** — stellar/terrestrial exploration; sky calculations, geographic indices and typed mathematical correspondences.

- **The Founder Office / Common Room** — a beautiful fictional social and administrative space for questions, collaboration and future invitations, with text-only interactivity initially.

Each space has spatial navigation, keyboard/touch equivalents, visible labels on the world itself and accessible direct web routes. Direct routes must load the named scene without replaying the entire introduction.

## B3. First interactive challenge: what is one plus one?

At the gate, implement a three-stage **Arithmetic of Possibility** sequence with genuine mathematical definitions:

1. A visitor chooses a number system (natural numbers, ordinary integers, or the two-element field); ask `1 + 1`, then compute and explain why the answer can be `2` in one structure and `0` in `F₂`.
2. Ask `0 / 1`; show why its quotient is `0` over ordinary fields. Compare `1 / 0` (no multiplicative solution) and `0 / 0` (nonunique inverse relation) without declaring either valid field division. Teach preimage and kernel concepts through a moving diagram. JavaScript's `Infinity`/`NaN` values are implementation representations, not substitute definitions of ordinary division.
3. Allow a transformation of a simple matrix or graph. Ask which information survives, then open the first division door by *demonstrating* the transformation's image and kernel.

The puzzle is playful, not an exclusionary intelligence test: every answer yields instruction, and visitors can continue. Multiple solutions are welcome where the chosen structure admits them. The site can theatrically "stupefy" with a brilliant visual effect, but never intimidates a visitor or claims those who fail lack intelligence.

## B4. Not a passive 3D background

The world must change in ways tied to mathematics:

- An accepted matrix operation creates a visible image-space sculpture and moves an inscription.
- A fold in Plicastratum compresses a walkway/path into one navigable glyph that can be unfolded into its constituent path.
- A graph transformation changes bridges and doors only where a typed admissibility predicate passes.
- A music interval changes light and architectural repetitions based on explicit modular/pitch relationships.
- Discovering a shared topological route can create a fictional portal and properly labeled spatial projection.

Every interaction implements an inspectable computation using lightweight browser-local functions; effect/visual transition must not invent numerical evidence. The resulting state is saved locally or (opt-in) to Supabase. The viewer can always reach the same subject as a normal web page if WebGL is unavailable.

# D. ONE-DAY HARNESS → SIX PERSONAL VOLUMES

This is the campus's flagship loop and must actually work as a client-side mechanism.

## D1. Enter a one-day harness

An individual may enter freely, without payment. They choose a public display name or remain private, opt into progress storage or keep it local, choose an intellectual interest, and start a self-paced **One-Day Harness**. It is defined as a guided one-day learning/production experience, not as a legal degree, verified attendance credential, automatic appointment, or promise that six fields have been mastered.

A progression across six spaces exposes real ideas, short exercises, interactive geometries, expressive writing and one user-derived contribution. A harness can be completed in a long visit or in connected sessions on the same day. Users may pause and resume through local browser storage; opt-in Supabase sync if login is configured.

## D2. The bookmaking miracle—actually implemented

At the completion of the one-day harness, produce **six distinct books, one for each division, credited to the participant by their chosen name**. Each book has a title generated deterministically from the chosen topic and subject glossary, an opening letter, division introduction, authentic mathematical foundation, at least one worked equation or diagram, the learner's own answer or reflection and its derivation, a usable glossary, citations, a generated visual plate, bibliography/links, and a transparent chapter on what the participant did versus what was sourced or computed. A work may be a concise *volume*, but it must contain substantive paragraphs and derivations, not repeated filler or a fabricated personal achievement. Honor anonymity and the ability to regenerate/rename without broadcasting personal data.

A client-side engine composes these volumes from vetted subject templates, actual computations, user inputs, and typed narrative grammars. Use `pdf-lib` or a comparably browser-capable library for real downloadable PDF exports, plus accessible HTML reader and optional printable format. Embed permitted fonts and use correct typographic hierarchy. PDFs are *generated in the browser*; user files remain local unless they opt into storing approved versions. Each book has source/credit metadata and a stable local ID. When a subject was only visited briefly, describe its encounter rather than claiming mastery.

**Authorship rule:** the participant authors their recorded thoughts and choices. Founding works are credited to their actual authors. University-generated explanations are identified as generated educational material; co-authorship is never falsely assigned to a participant or an AI/person without a concrete contribution and consent. Fictional invented terms are labeled as constructions.

## D3. The Library that appears already immense

The 3D Library contains **many dynamically constructed specimen books**, corresponding to the real subject registry and deterministic combinations of topics. They may glow, fold, form constellations of reference and lead to a lesson. Their displays clearly say `Specimen Volume / Preview`, distinct from a real participant's verified book. Public participant books appear only when deliberately published with consent. Counts of real books or completed harnesses come solely from actual stored records, rather than invented progress badges. The space should nevertheless feel alive and dense on day one.

## D4. Harness Master contact route

Provide a clear mechanism to *request* one free conversation or link to a verified contact/booking path when configured; avoid fake appointment times, private phone numbers or unapproved outgoing messages. If no scheduling provider is set up, show a concise, functioning request draft / copy contact flow, clearly marked as a request rather than confirmed booking. The website's primary self-guided harness remains fully functional without a human response.

# E. THE OBSERVATORY — EARTH, ADDRESSES, SKY, IMAGES

## E1. An address is a gateway to computational geography

The user can enter a place or address, and with explicit submission the client geocodes it through a permitted provider. Display the resulting approximate location and what provider/precision supplied it. The viewer may use known coordinates directly without requesting a third-party geocoder. Do not retain or publish the exact address automatically. Use MapLibre GL JS with licensed self-hosted/open vector tile sources and proper attribution, or a lightweight map/globe visual that does not breach public tile-host usage policies. Country/street availability reflects actual data coverage.

If a network provider is unavailable, offer an offline set of sample locations that are openly labeled examples. Do not pretend synthetic streets represent actual geographic data. Do not route sensitive real-person addresses to random services or log fine-grained coordinates in analytics.

## E2. Geometry of linked places

A sourced street path becomes a typed graph word of turns, junction degree, segment geometry and topology. Compute a signature and compare it against other indexed path candidates. Visually show the source paths, the selected normalization and the similarity metric. Compare exact versus approximate correspondences and mark unverified candidates. Two streets being geometrically similar **does not** imply a physical tunnel or causal connection. A fictional portal may visualize the *discovered mathematical correspondence*, and the user may choose `visit`, `overlay`, or `braid` as an authored experience in the campus scene.

## E3. Celestial representation

Calculate actual observer-dependent celestial positions from a pinned browser-compatible astronomical package and a license-appropriate small reference dataset; include observer position, selected time/epoch, time zone and coordinate-frame labels. Render a navigable sky with stars as points, dynamic camera orientation and original graphic accents. Distinguish genuine astronomical positions from authored constellation-like correspondences constructed for the campus. Show one explicit ground-to-sky mapping with its mathematical definition and declared loss. A successful example is repeatable by another user with the same inputs; incorrect or unavailable astronomy must be labeled, not decorated as a discovery.

## E4. Imagistratum, our first public exhibit but not our only subject

The mathematical `image` of a mapping is its reachable output set; demonstrate it with a linear transformation and a 3D/2D projected sculpture. Provide preimage, rank, null space, projection-loss and counterexample controls. Connect this idea visually to photo cameras, 3D scenes, graph images, sky charts, UI and the transformative site itself. Let a visitor learn about an image without mistaking the subject for only a bitmap.
